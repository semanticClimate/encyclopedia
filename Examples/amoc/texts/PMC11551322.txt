Significance Recent research on the modern Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) has unveiled a complex three-dimensional structure. However, most previous studies on the glacial AMOC have primarily concentrated on features associated with two-dimensional overturning. This study investigates the three-dimensional glacial AMOC by combining sedimentary 231 Pa/ 230 Th reconstructions with model simulations, revealing a substantially intensified Eastern Pathway (EP), which transports Glacial North Atlantic Intermediate Water (GNAIW) from subpolar to subtropical North Atlantic in the eastern basin. This glacial EP is driven by basin-scale open-ocean convection, in contrast to the wind-steering mechanism in the modern ocean. The elucidation of this dramatic change of the EP provides a more accurate view of the glacial AMOC and its transport of energy and water properties. Keywords: Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, Last Glacial Maximum, North Atlantic Deep Water, 231 Pa/ 230 Th, transport pathway Abstract Abundant proxy records suggest a profound reorganization of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) during the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM, ~21,000 y ago), with the North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW) shoaling significantly relative to the present-day (PD) and forming Glacial North Atlantic Intermediate Water (GNAIW). However, almost all previous observational and modeling studies have focused on the zonal mean two-dimensional AMOC feature, while recent progress in the understanding of modern AMOC reveals a more complicated three-dimensional structure, with NADW penetrating from the subpolar North Atlantic to lower latitude through different pathways. Here, combining 231 Pa/ 230 Th reconstructions and model simulations, we uncover a significant change in the three-dimensional structure of the glacial AMOC. Specifically, the mid-latitude eastern pathway (EP), located east of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and transporting about half of the PD NADW from the subpolar gyre to the subtropical gyre, experienced substantial intensification during the LGM. A greater portion of the GNAIW was transported in the eastern basin during the LGM compared to NADW at the PD, resulting in opposite 231 Pa/ 230 Th changes between eastern and western basins during the LGM. Furthermore, in contrast to the wind-steering mechanism of EP at PD, the intensified LGM EP was caused primarily by the rim current forced by the basin-scale open-ocean convection over the subpolar North Atlantic. Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) is a key player in climate change due to its role in redistributing heat, carbon, and nutrients in the ocean. AMOC experienced a dramatic change during the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM, ~21,000 y ago) with a substantial shoaling of the North Atlantic Deep Water (NADW), forming the so-called Glacial North Atlantic Intermediate Water (GNAIW) ( 1 , 2 ). Numerous observational and modeling studies have been carried out on the LGM AMOC. However, almost all the studies have focused on the features associated with the zonal mean two-dimensional overturning circulation ( 1 – 5 ). Yet, a recent synthesis of North Atlantic 231 Pa/ 230 Th, a kinematic proxy for ocean transport ( Materials and Methods ), shows systematically lower 231 Pa/ 230 Th in the eastern basin and higher 231 Pa/ 230 Th in the western basin at the LGM than the present day (PD) ( 6 ). It has been speculated that this opposite 231 Pa/ 230 Th response between the western and eastern basins reflects a reduced southward transport in the western basin and increased southward transport in the eastern basin ( 6 ). A recent modeling study also shows a stronger southward transport in the eastern basin at LGM than at PD ( 7 ). At PD, NADW consists of a mixture of the Labrador Sea Water (LSW) and the overflow waters from the Greenland-Iceland-Norwegian (GIN) Seas and is transported from the subpolar to subtropical North Atlantic through different pathways. The classical paradigm of the southward NADW route is along the deep western boundary ( 8 , 9 ). However, Lagrangian floats reveal an eddy-driven interior pathway west of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge (MAR) ( 10 – 14 ). Furthermore, recent studies based on Argo data ( 15 ), ocean reanalysis ( 16 ), and models with a hierarchy of resolutions ( 17 ) suggest that a significant portion of NADW is transported from the subpolar region to the subtropics in the Eastern Pathway (EP) east of MAR at mid-latitude that is steered by surface winds ( 17 ). The complex NADW pathways at PD and the reconstructed east–west contrast of the 231 Pa/ 230 Th response in the mid-latitude North Atlantic at LGM ( 6 ) further raise a fundamental question: What is the response and mechanism of the three-dimensional structure of AMOC pathway, especially the EP, in the past? Here, we study this question using an ocean general circulation model, iPOP2 ( Materials and Methods ), that simulates the EP at PD in agreement with observations ( 17 ). Our model also incorporates multiple proxies ( Materials and Methods ), enabling a direct model-data comparison with paleo observations. We find that the EP was greatly intensified at the LGM, resulting in opposite 231 Pa/ 230 Th changes between the eastern and western Atlantic consistent with reconstructions. Furthermore, in contrast to the wind-steering mechanism at PD, the EP at LGM was mainly driven by the buoyancy-induced basin-scale open-ocean convection in the subpolar North Atlantic. Simulated LGM Ocean State Under LGM surface forcing, the simulated water masses and proxies that are associated with the AMOC agree well with observations ( 5 ), especially the shoaling of the NADW at LGM ( Fig. 1 A and E ). At the LGM, the subpolar and subtropical gyres are intensified significantly, with a decrease of the southwest-northeast tilt of the intergyre boundary, such that the boundary is shifted southward to along ~40 o N ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 C ). This change in the subpolar/subtropical gyres is caused by the intensification and southward shift of the westerly wind across the North Atlantic forced by the Laurentide Ice Sheet ( 18 ). In contrast to the PD, when the NADW consists of LSW and GIN Seas overflow waters, the GNAIW originates predominantly from the subpolar North Atlantic, as indicated by idealized dye tracers in the model ( Materials and Methods ) ( SI Appendix , Fig. S2 ). This shift of the deep water formation region from the GIN Seas and Labrador Sea in PD to the subpolar North Atlantic in LGM is associated with greater expansion of the winter sea ice ( Fig. 1 F ), consistent qualitatively with sea ice reconstructions ( 19 – 21 ). At the LGM, the GIN Seas were almost fully covered with sea ice, which inhibited deep convection. Meanwhile, the western subpolar North Atlantic was partly covered with sea ice. The brine rejection associated with the sea ice expansion over the western subpolar North Atlantic and the southward migration of the precipitation associated with the Atlantic storm track ( 18 , 22 ) increased surface salinity and, therefore, density in the subpolar North Atlantic open ocean ( Fig. 1 G ). Besides, the katabatic winds associated with the Laurentide Ice sheet may also contribute to high winter surface density in the subpolar North Atlantic. The simulated high surface density in the subpolar North Atlantic open ocean is consistent with winter surface density estimation based on glacial surface reconstructions, which shows maximum density near 40°W in a similar location as in LGM simulation ( 23 , 24 ), although the LGM simulation may somewhat overestimate the area of high surface density relative to the glacial surface estimation ( SI Appendix , Fig. S3 C and D ). Enhanced Eastern Pathway of GNAIW At PD, the simulated upper NADW is transported across the intergyre boundary at 45°N in the EP ( Figs. 1 C and D and 2 A ), in agreement with hydrographic and Argo observations, ocean reanalysis, and eddy-resolving simulations ( 17 ), although the eddy-driven western interior pathways ( 10 , 13 , 14 ) are not accurately captured due to the 3-degree coarse resolution ( Materials and Methods ). The western interior pathway is related to the homogeneous potential vorticity (PV) by eddy activity and therefore can only be captured correctly in eddy-resolving simulations ( 17 ). However, the EP is formed by the large-scale surface wind-steering on the intergyre baroclinic communication window between subpolar gyre and subtropical gyre ( 17 , 25 – 27 ), instead of eddies, and therefore can be simulated in both eddy-resolving and non-eddy-resolving simulations. The overall consistency of the location and magnitude of the EP between the coarse resolution model and observations, as well as eddy-resolving models ( SI Appendix , Fig. S4 ) ( 17 ), warrants the application of the coarse resolution model to the study of the EP at LGM. The LGM EP is greatly enhanced from the PD with the stronger southward transport and a slight eastward shift by ~7° ( Figs. 2 and 3 A and SI Appendix , Fig. S5 G ). This enhanced LGM EP is further supported by a set of ocean simulations with 1-degree resolution under the same surface forcings ( Materials and Methods ). The consistent EP changes simulated by both the 1-degree and 3-degree models, together with the 231 Pa/ 230 Th reconstructions, suggest a robust intensification of EP at LGM. Buoyancy Forcing for LGM Eastern Pathway The mid-latitude EP at PD is formed fundamentally as the baroclinic communication window ( 25 – 27 ) steered by large-scale surface wind ( 17 ), although it may be modified modestly by the MAR topography ( 16 ). As such, at the PD, a suppression of mid-latitude wind shifts the NADW southward export to within the western basin ( 17 ) ( Fig. 4 B ). In contrast, the EP at LGM remains robust when the wind forcing is suppressed, as shown in two sensitivity experiments LGM_nowind and LGM_nowind_FB ( Materials and Methods ) ( Fig. 1 I – P ). This suggests that wind is not the driving mechanism for EP at LGM. In all three LGM experiments, regardless of the topographic effect (LGM_nowind_FB) and wind effect (LGM_nowind), the southward EP at the intergyre boundary near 40 o N remains at around ~20 o W ( Figs. 1 , 2 , and 4 ). The LGM EP is primarily formed as part of the rim-current associated with the buoyancy-driven basin-scale open-ocean convection, illustrated most clearly in LGM_nowind_FB. At LGM, open-ocean convection, spanning over the western half of the subpolar North Atlantic due to surface cooling and sea ice expansion ( Fig. 1 F, J , and N and SI Appendix , Fig. S6 ), forms a giant dome of dense and low PV water, all the way down to the mid-depths, which extends to the western boundary due to Rossby wave propagation in the so-called beta-plume ( 30 , 31 ) ( Fig. 4 C–E ). This low PV dome is accompanied by a gyre-scale baroclinic “rim” current that is cyclonic in the upper ocean and anticyclonic in the mid-depth ( 32 – 34 ) ( Fig. 4 F – H and SI Appendix , Fig. S7 ). The mid-depth anticyclonic “rim” current first transports the low PV water eastward along the northern boundary (~60 o N), forming the downwelling branch of the AMOC ( SI Appendix , Fig. S8 and Supplementary Information text ); the low PV water then flows southward and downward adiabatically on sloping isopycnals along the eastern rim of the pool (~20 o W), forming the EP ( Fig. 4 C–E and SI Appendix , Fig. S9 ). With neither bottom topography nor wind, the rim current system is purely baroclinic such that the mid-depth anticyclonic circulation is completely compensated by the upper layer cyclonic circulation ( Fig. 4 F – H ), leaving no barotropic transport ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 F ). Bottom topography in LGM_nowind distorts the baroclinic rim current substantially via the joint effect of baroclinicity and bottom relief (JEBAR) effect ( 35 ), such that the eastward transport in the mid-depth is shifted southward from along the northern boundary (~60 o N) to the mid-gyre (~50 o N) by the sloping Greenland-Scotland ridge ( Fig. 4 C and D and SI Appendix , Figs. S7 and S9 ); nevertheless, the southward EP along the eastern rim of the pool remains unchanged ( Fig. 4 C – E ). Wind forcing in LGM leads to a further strengthening and eastward shift of the EP from LGM_nowind ( Fig. 2 ), consistent with the wind-driven mechanism of EP at PD ( 17 ). However, at the LGM, the buoyancy effect is much stronger than the wind effect, such that the buoyancy forcing alone is able to generate the EP in LGM_nowind and LGM_nowind_FB. In contrast, at PD, the weak buoyancy forcing confines the open-ocean convection in the northwestern Atlantic to the Labrador Sea and Irminger Sea ( 36 , 37 ) and the intermediate water thus generated cannot penetrate southward in the interior ocean unless it is advected by a strong wind-driven subpolar gyre eastward into the mid-basin at mid-depth. The enhanced transport in the eastern basin at LGM was attributed to a weaker AMOC overturning (by 50%) and the resultant weakening in zonal density gradient in the LOVECLIM LGM simulation compared to PD ( 7 ). However, in our model, the LGM EP is enhanced compared to PD in both slightly stronger AMOC case (Experiment LGM) and much weaker AMOC case (Experiment LGM_weak, Materials and Methods ) ( SI Appendix , Fig. S5 F and G ). This suggests that the strength of AMOC is not necessarily the dominant cause for the enhanced eastern basin transport. Instead, the strong surface cooling and sea ice expansion over the subpolar North Atlantic are robust features of the LGM in both our simulations with different model resolutions ( Fig. 1 and SI Appendix , Fig. S6 ) and in the LOVECLIM model ( 7 ), as well as in state estimation based on reconstructions ( 23 , 24 )( SI Appendix , Fig. S3 ), offering a more robust mechanism for the enhanced EP transport at LGM. Implications for Reconstructing Past AMOC Changes Our study suggests that the east–west contrast in the mid-latitude North Atlantic 231 Pa/ 230 Th reconstructions is associated with the intensified EP at LGM. The 231 Pa/ 230 Th proxy is sensitive to the regional change of deep North Atlantic flow. Even a modest eastward shift of the EP from LGM_nowind to LGM shows a zonal contrasting response of 231 Pa/ 230 Th in the mid-latitude ( SI Appendix , Fig. S10 ). This ability of 231 Pa/ 230 Th, a kinematic proxy, to detect regional circulation changes may be unique compared with other water mass proxies such as δ 13 C . In addition to the caveat that δ 13 C is affected by remineralization, δ 13 C tends to be well mixed by circulation along isopycnal surfaces, with relatively uniform values in the subtropical gyre, and δ 13 C shows significant smaller zonal variation relative to latitudinal variation than 231 Pa/ 230 Th in the mid-latitude North Atlantic ( SI Appendix , Fig. S11 ), suggesting that 231 Pa/ 230 Th is more sensitive for detecting changes of zonal flow patterns. The capability of 231 Pa/ 230 Th to detect regional circulation changes provides an exciting opportunity to study the response of the AMOC three-dimensional structure to climate change in the past, beyond the zonal mean overturning circulation perspective. The intensified EP by buoyancy-driven open-ocean convection at LGM is also important for an accurate, in-depth reconstruction of LGM AMOC. Different proxy reconstructions reveal a robust glacial shoaling of AMOC ( 1 , 38 ), but the AMOC strength inferred from 231 Pa/ 230 Th reconstructions at LGM relative to PD is still uncertain ( 39 – 44 ). The change of AMOC geometry at LGM may complicate the interpretation of the 231 Pa/ 230 Th records ( 5 ), which is now further complicated by the GNAIW route change at the mid-latitude as revealed by the opposite changes of 231 Pa/ 230 Th records between eastern and western basin ( 6 ). Therefore, simple observational assessment of individual 231 Pa/ 230 Th records may not provide an accurate picture of past AMOC. The combined approach utilizing both isotope-enabled simulations and 231 Pa/ 230 Th records may provide a more accurate reconstruction of past AMOC. In addition, the intensified EP also leads to zonally different changes in tracers, such as water age and oxygen concentration ( SI Appendix , Fig. S12 ). Therefore, considering the zonal circulation changes will help to improve the interpretation of mid-latitude proxy records. Our study highlights the importance of recognizing the three-dimensional changes for the accurate reconstruction of past ocean circulation. Materials and Methods Isotope Enabled Parallel Ocean Program 2 (iPOP2). Parallel Ocean Program (POP2) ( 45 ) is the ocean component of the Community Earth System Model (CESM). The ocean model configuration is nominal 3° horizontal resolution and 60 vertical layers, with 10 m resolution in the upper 200 m, expanding to 250 m resolution below 3,000 m. POP2 adopts hydrostatic approximation and the convection in POP2 is treated as enhanced vertical mixing. When vertical density profile indicates convection, large diffusivity and viscosity are applied ( 46 ). Modified K-profile Parameterization (KPP) ( 47 ) is used for vertical mixing and diffusivity is approximately 0.1 × 10 −4 m 2 /s near the surface and increasing to 1 × 10 −4 m 2 /s to the depth ( 48 ). The simulated modern deep convection sites using POP2 broadly agrees with modern observations ( 45 ). For direct model-data comparison, isotope-enabled POP2 (iPOP2) is developed with the implementation of the several most commonly used isotope tracers: carbon isotopes ( 49 ), 231 Pa/ 230 Th ( 50 ), δ 18 O ( 51 ), and neodymium isotopes ( 52 ). To illustrate the role of different NADW source regions, dye tracers are implemented in the model. A dye tracer is released with 0 surface value except in the source region where its surface value is specified at 1. In the interior ocean, dye tracers are transported as other passive tracers. Two dye tracers are released, one from the GIN Seas (including the Irminger Sea and Iceland basin) and the other from the subpolar Atlantic Ocean, including the Labrador Sea ( SI Appendix , Fig. S2 A ). PD Simulations. The ocean model iPOP2 is coupled to data models of atmosphere, sea ice, land, and river from the normal year forcing in CORE-II dataset ( 53 ) for the PD simulation. The temperature is forced by the surface heat flux calculated from the observed atmospheric variables and model SST via the bulk formula, while salinity is forced on the surface by the observed precipitation minus evaporation flux with an additional weak salinity restoring of 4-y time scale. The experiment uses realistic topography ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 B ). The PD experiment is initialized from a 7,000-y spin up and integrated for another 1,600 y. The average of the last 100 y is used as PD in this study. The maximum AMOC transport is 15.6 Sv ( Fig. 1 A ), with the deep convection occurring mainly in GIN Seas, Irminger Sea, and Iceland Basin ( Fig. 1 B ), consistent with observations ( 37 , 54 ). The barotropic streamfunction shows a standard double-gyre structure of subtropical and subpolar gyres over the North Atlantic with a tilted intergyre boundary along ~45°N ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 ). The vertical NADW extent is overall consistent with observations, as indicated in the model-data comparison of water mass proxies such as δ 13 C ( 49 ) and ε Nd ( 52 ). To illustrate the wind effect on EP at PD, the Experiment PD_nowind is presented. The model set-up for PD_nowind is the same as PD, except that the surface wind over 15°N to 90°N in the North Atlantic is suppressed to 0.01 of the original value. This experiment is run for 1,000 y and the average of the last 10 y in this simulation is used for analysis. LGM Experiments. We present five experiments under the LGM condition using ocean-alone iPOP2. All five experiments are forced by monthly surface forcings (heat flux, freshwater flux, and momentum flux) from a coupled model LGM simulation (TRACE21K) ( 55 ), with a 30-d restoring to monthly surface salinity in TRACE21K and 10-d restoring to monthly surface temperature in TRACE21K. Details of this forcing method are described in references ( 51 , 56 ). (1) Experiment LGM is taken from a transient ocean simulation of the past 22,000 y with active biogeochemical cycles and multiple isotopes (C-iTRACE) ( 56 ). The bottom topography uses the PD topography after lowering the sea level by 120 m ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 E ). The average from 19.1 ka to 19.0 ka in C-iTRACE is analyzed as LGM in this study. TRACE21K applies the land ice sheet reconstruction by ICE-5G ( 57 ). Therefore, the impact of elevated continental ice sheet (e.g., the Laurentide Ice Sheet) on atmospheric circulation is considered in the model. However, the katabatic winds associated with the Laurentide Ice Sheet might be poorly resolved due to coarse resolution ( 58 ) and worth detailed studies in high-resolution models in the future. The barotropic streamfunction shows an intensified double-gyre with the intergyre boundary shifted from ~ 45°N at PD southward to ~40 o N ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 C ). The AMOC maximum streamfunction is 17.6 Sv ( Fig. 1 E ), which is slightly stronger than PD and the AMOC depth is significantly shallower than the PD. Previous theoretical studies suggest that the AMOC strength is correlated with AMOC depth following the scaling law: T NA ∼ Δ ρ H 2 f n , where T NA is the AMOC strength, H is AMOC depth, f n is the Coriolis parameter at the latitude where NADW forms, and Δ ρ is the meridional density gradient or the shared density range between circumpolar channel and the isopycnal outcrop region in the North Atlantic ( 59 , 60 ). However, the direct application of this scaling law to the deglacial AMOC change is complicated by the choice of Δ ρ ( 61 ). A full understanding of the relationship between the depth and strength of the AMOC during the last deglaciation remains to be studied in the future. (2) Experiment LGM_nowind is the same as experiment LGM but with suppressed wind forcing over the North Atlantic. The experiment is initiated from 22 ka in C-iTRACE and integrated for 1,000 y, forced by the 22 ka to 21 ka surface forcings in TRACE21K in the same way as C-iTRACE, except that the surface wind stress is reduced to 0.01 of the original values over 15°N to 90°N in the North Atlantic, including the Labrador Sea. The average of the last 10 y in this simulation is used for analysis. The AMOC streamfunction remains similar to LGM ( Fig. 1 I ), with a maximum transport of 20.1 Sv. In the barotropic streamfunction, the subtropical gyre disappears, but the subpolar gyre remains modestly strong ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 D ) because of the bottom topography and JEBAR effect ( 35 ). One thing to note is that the wind effect discussed in this study is in the context of ocean alone model with the surface buoyancy forcing fixed and the surface buoyancy feedback by the wind is therefore not considered, which is different from the wind effect in coupled climate models ( 62 ) ( SI Appendix, Text ). (3) Experiment LGM_nowind_FB is the same as LGM_nowind but with a flat bottom set at the depth of 5,375 m uniformly north of 15°N in the North Atlantic ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 G ). The experiment is integrated for 1,000 y and the average of the last 10 y in this simulation is used for analysis. The AMOC streamfunction remains similar to LGM, with a maximum transport of 19.8 Sv ( Fig. 1 M ). Now, both subtropical and subpolar gyres vanish in the barotropic streamfunction ( SI Appendix , Fig. S1 F ), as expected from the Sverdrup relation. (4) Experiment LGM_weak is the decadal average of the 17.5 ka state in C-iTRACE. Since the freshwater forcing is gradually added in C-iTRACE, ramping from 3 m/kyr to 5 m/kyr from 18.4 ka to 17.5 ka, the model can be treated as in quasi-equilibrium. In LGM_weak, the simulated AMOC transport is 9.9 Sv, which is about half of the AMOC transport at LGM, while the geometry of AMOC in LGM_weak is similar to LGM ( 5 ) ( SI Appendix , Fig. S5 ). The surface condition is also similar to LGM as the open-ocean convection spanning the western half of the subpolar North Atlantic still presents ( SI Appendix , Fig. S5 B and C ). Therefore, LGM_weak is a sensitivity test for the GNAIW pathway with a different AMOC strength. (5) Experiment LGM_NA&SO_nowind is the same as experiment LGM_nowind but with additional suppressed wind forcing over the Southern Ocean. The surface wind stress is set to 0.01 of the original values over both North Atlantic and Southern Ocean. The experiment is initiated from LGM_nowind and integrated for 1,000 y. The average of the last 10 y in this simulation is used for analysis. The AMOC transport is reduced to 13.1 Sv, much weaker than LGM and LGM_nowind, which confirms that the wind stress over the Southern Ocean acts as the “pulling” effect that drives the adiabatic circulation of AMOC ( 59 , 63 , 64 ). However, albeit weaker, the EP stays in the similar location as in LGM and LGM_nowind because the open ocean convection still presents due to the fixed surface buoyancy forcing ( SI Appendix , Fig. S13 ). PD and LGM Ocean Simulations with 1-degree Resolution. To verify the results from 3° simulations, a set of 1° simulations are carried out under PD and LGM condition. The 1° simulations share the same model set-up as the 3° simulations, except for the model resolution. The PD is forced by CORE-II dataset ( 53 ) and the LGM is forced by monthly surface forcings from TRACE21K simulation ( 55 ). The simulated mid-depth NADW pathways at the intergyre boundary under PD condition have been compared with the 0.1° high-resolution simulation ( 65 ), which reveals that unlike the 3° simulations in which the NADW is excessively transported in the EP, some of the NADW is also transported in the interior pathway west of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, in better agreement with the 0.1° high-resolution simulation ( 17 ) ( SI Appendix , Fig. S4 ). The simulated North Atlantic deep convection regions and the EP features in the 1° simulations are all similar to the 3° simulations under both PD ( 17 ) and LGM condition ( SI Appendix , Fig. S6 ). Previous studies suggest that low-resolution models tend to simulate excessive open ocean convections, especially around the Antarctica ( 66 ). There is also a possibility that the open ocean convection in the glacial North Atlantic simulated in the 3°, and even the 1°, simulations might be biased. Here, under PD condition, the deep convection regions in the North Atlantic overall agrees with the very high-resolution eddy-resolving (0.1°) simulation ( 65 ). This agreement gives some credential to our model simulations for the North Atlantic. In addition to the reasonable model performance for the present day, the simulated regions of deep convections in the LGM subpolar North Atlantic agrees with the glacial winter surface density reconstruction. Therefore, the open ocean convection in the LGM subpolar North Atlantic might be a robust feature of the LGM ocean state. Nevertheless, it is certainly desirable in the future to perform the simulations to explore the open ocean convection and its associated rim currents in more advanced models with higher resolution and, even better, without the hydrostatic approximation that is adopted in the current generation of CESM. Observational and Model 231 Pa/ 230 Th. 231 Pa/ 230 Th is a kinematic proxy for ocean transport. Both 231 Pa and 230 Th are produced at relatively uniform rates in the ocean with a production ratio of 0.093, and the isotopes are subject to ocean transport and particle scavenging ( 67 ). The different scavenging efficiency of the two isotopes leads to different residence times between 231 Pa and 230 Th: approximately 50 to 200 y for 231 Pa and 10 to 40 y for 230 Th ( 42 , 67 ). In the North Atlantic, the longer residence time of 231 Pa allows a greater amount of the isotope to be exported southward by deep ocean currents, resulting in widespread observations of modern-day sediment 231 Pa/ 230 Th below the production ratio (~0.093) at depth. Based on the principle above, a positive shift in the deep North Atlantic downcore sediment 231 Pa/ 230 Th is often interpreted as weaker ocean transport and vice versa ( 39 ). However, in addition to ocean transport, sediment 231 Pa/ 230 Th can also be influenced by particle flux and composition ( 68 ). Following the criteria set by a previous compilation of available North Atlantic 231 Pa/ 230 Th sediment core observations ( 6 ), sediment records that were considered to have a dominant influence by particle fluxes were not included in the model-observation comparison in this study ( SI Appendix , Table S1 ). 231 Pa/ 230 Th in iPOP2 is implemented in two versions: p-coupled and p-fixed ( 50 ). In p-coupled 231 Pa/ 230 Th, the particle fluxes associated with reversible scavenging are simultaneously calculated from the biogeochemical model. In p-fixed 231 Pa/ 230 Th, the particle fluxes are fixed at the PD values produced by the biogeochemical model in iPOP2. In this study, the P -fixed version of 231 Pa/ 230 Th is used, and the 231 Pa/ 230 Th change in the model is only associated with circulation change. The simulated PD 231 Pa/ 230 Th is in agreement with core-top observations ( 50 ), and the simulated LGM 231 Pa/ 230 Th captures the broad feature of LGM Atlantic 231 Pa/ 230 Th observations ( 5 ). The presence of some discrepancies between observed and modeled 231 Pa/ 230 Th ( Fig. 3 B ) is probably due to a combination of the coarse model resolution, sediment chronology uncertainty, and additional oceanic processes influencing 231 Pa/ 230 Th that are recently discovered ( 69 ). Despite some discrepancies in the absolute values, both the model and observations show a consistent east–west contrast in the difference of 231 Pa/ 230 Th between LGM and PD ( Fig. 3 B ), lending support to the change in the three-dimensional structure of AMOC between these two periods.
